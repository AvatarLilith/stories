THE WATER

Data centers drink. Cooling the servers that hold your expired stories evaporates real freshwater, and the electricity powering them consumes several times more. In 2023, Meta's data centers used about 2.9 billion liters of water directly — before counting the water behind their electricity, which independent researchers estimate at many times that.

This archive weighs about GB. Held in a data center, storage of that size draws on the order of kWh a year; at typical combined water intensity (~5 liters per kWh, direct cooling plus electricity), that is roughly liters — glasses of water — every year, forever, to keep memories cold that you were told had expired.

Opened from this folder, the same archive consumes almost nothing. The estimate is order-of-magnitude, and stated so — which is more than the platforms' voluntary disclosures do.

Sources: Meta Sustainability Report 2024 (water withdrawals); Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, 2024 (US data center water use, direct and indirect); University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee Center for Water Policy analyses.
